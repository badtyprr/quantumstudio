## Historical performance diagnosis

At the original audit revision, the unchanged inverse-QFT QASM, including all three measurements, imported successfully but exceeded a 60-second worker limit in exact-circuit mode. The ideal inverse Fourier transform gives uniform zero-input probabilities, 1/8 each. The generated program uses rounded decimal angles, so its literal probabilities need not equal 1/8 exactly. The complete execution now passes in the release documented above.

The same unchanged program passed numerical trajectory sampling: 10,000 shots with seed 1731 completed in approximately 0.375 seconds. Counts for `000` through `111` were 1213, 1254, 1320, 1236, 1243, 1244, 1262 and 1228. The largest frequency deviation from 1/8 was 0.007, below the preselected 0.02 tolerance. This verifies the sampled measurement route for this input, not exact-mode completion or an exact probability result.

A bounded diagnostic retained the original decimal angles and appended the original terminal measurements to gate prefixes. One, two and six gates completed in about 0.11, 0.06 and 0.09 seconds; 18 and 55 gates each exceeded five seconds. Six gates already produced long symbolic trigonometric expressions. This supports investigating expression growth during exact evaluation of decimal-angle decompositions; it does not establish a complete performance diagnosis.

- Prioritize a scoped P1 investigation of exact-circuit expression growth for this reproducible algorithm input. Preserve the distinction between literal decimals and exact multiples of pi; silently rounding angles would change the program.
- Acceptance: evaluate the unchanged full measurement program within an explicit practical resource budget, verify all eight outcomes against an independent reference retaining the imported literal angles, and check proximity to the ideal 1/8 distribution separately. Preserve exact normalization and keep numerical and exact semantics explicit. A numerical execution result alone does not close exact-mode coverage.
- DEV-13/15 still cover in-app notebook/SDK and provider execution. Editor import/export and GUI round trips remain untested for this fixture.
- Keep QB-005 unchecked until the remaining cells and routes have evidence or explicit supported alternatives.

## Exact-execution investigation, 2026-10-24

The published `5b982f1f` worker still exceeded a three-second diagnostic limit on the full imported program. Timers in a separate diagnostic build located the dominant cost in symbolic gate application: a 15-gate prefix spent approximately 2.71 seconds executing, 0.12 seconds calculating output weights, and 0.02 seconds formatting. The full program timed out during gate application at 12 seconds. Disabling expression expansion was rejected: the same prefix took about 7.45 seconds and produced 90 KB of output. These timings identify an implementation area, not a completed fix or a portable CI threshold.

A separate Python feasibility experiment represented each amplitude as an exact integer-coefficient sum of rational-angle phases, with explicit powers of two and square roots of two for normalization. It consumed the current frozen importer's gate expressions without rounding their angles to pi. All 55 unitary gates completed in about 0.94 seconds, producing 28,167 amplitude terms. Expanding all eight squared amplitudes took about 35 seconds overall and produced 22,815–22,989 terms per outcome. Their sum cancelled algebraically to exactly one.

An independently implemented dense-matrix calculation using mpmath 1.3.0 at 60 decimal digits agreed with each saved probability polynomial to better than `1e-50`. The largest deviation from ideal 1/8 was approximately `7.38e-17`. For example, computational-basis state `001` had probability approximately `0.1250000000000000737654995020481499`; the source's measurement destinations and selected classical-bit order map it to output label `100`. These checks cover the imported literal gate expressions from zero input. They do not claim that the shipped exact executor completed, that arbitrary circuits are supported by the experiment, or that the GUI can handle these expanded results.

This diagnosis motivated the bounded execution and result-display changes now shipped above. The acceptance required explicit operation/term budgets, cancellation, supported-domain checks, a fallback for other domains and the full unchanged source through the worker interface, without substituting a numerical-only result or an idealized Fourier circuit.

Local reproduction helpers are `.tools/probe-qb005-phase-polynomial.py` and `.tools/verify-qb005-phase-polynomial.py`; evidence is retained under `.tools/qb005-phase-polynomial/`. They are investigation tooling outside the tracked repository, not new CI tests or a shipping backend. One scoped self-review corrected a medium-severity ambiguity between basis order and classical output order; no findings remain outstanding. This was not an independent review.
